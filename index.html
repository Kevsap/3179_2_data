A data story about business events in Australia

Lanyards & Luggage

The $17 billion visitors you never notice

Every year, millions of people travel across Australia, and from overseas, to attend a conference, an exhibition or a work meeting. They fill our hotels, eat in our restaurants and fly between our cities. This page follows where they come from, where they go, and how they came back after COVID.

Chapter 1 · The size of it

A quiet industry worth $17 billion

When we think of tourism, we picture holidays. But a large share of travel happens because of work. A business event is any of the following:

Chapter 2 · 35 years at the border

Built over three decades, lost in a month

Every visitor to Australia states the main reason for their trip on arrival. The Australian Bureau of Statistics has counted the visitors who come for a convention or conference every month since 1991. The record shows steady growth, a few shocks, and one collapse.

Chapter 3 · Where they come from

Our nearest neighbour sends the most delegates

New Zealand sends more business event visitors than any other country. But numbers are not everything: visitors from further away stay longer and spend more.

Chapter 4 · Where they meet

Big cities earn the most, small capitals need it most

Sydney and Melbourne collect the largest amounts. But for Canberra and Darwin, business events make up a far bigger part of their whole tourism economy.

Chapter 5 · The global race

Australia competes with the world for every conference

International associations move their annual congress to a new country each year, and cities bid against each other to host them. The International Congress and Convention Association (ICCA) ranks cities by how many of these meetings they hold.

Chapter 6 · What it means for you

Three things to remember

01

It is bigger than it looks

Business event visitors spent $17.2 billion in 2025, mostly on hotels, meals and transport, money that supports local jobs well beyond the convention centre.

02

The recovery is not finished

Conference arrivals are at 88% of their pre-COVID level and business arrivals at 72%, while holiday and family travel are nearly back to normal.

03

Every city is in the race

Smaller capitals rely on business events the most, and Australian cities are competing with the world to win them. Brisbane's rise shows rankings can change quickly.

Notes on the data

  • TRA changed how it measures domestic travel from January 2025, adding mobility data. Domestic results for 2025 should not be compared directly with earlier years; international results are unaffected.
  • TRA's dashboard does not allow downloads, so its figures were transcribed exactly as published.
  • ABS figures count border crossings, not people, and only the main reason for the trip. A delegate who ticked "holiday" is not counted as a conference visitor.
  • ABS public data does not split reason for travel by country or state, so the maps use TRA data instead.
  • ICCA counts only international association meetings that rotate between countries, not corporate events. Meeting counts were only published for 2019 and 2022, so the bump chart shows rank.
  • All dollar values are nominal (not adjusted for inflation).
